    How projections work

    A transparent, usage-first model. No black box.

    Priority order

    Every projection and start/sit call is built in this order: 1. Vegas and market signals, 2. opportunity, 3. efficiency over expected, 4. context, then box-score averages. Weekly decisions weight the recent role and this week's betting environment most heavily.

    1 · Vegas & market

    The game spread and total come from DraftKings, via ESPN. If the books haven't posted yet, the app falls back to the nflverse schedule line. Implied team total = total ÷ 2 − team spread ÷ 2. The model compares that number with its own expected team points (offense scoring × opponent points allowed, regressed to league average). The ratio scales TD rates (square root) and yardage (power 0.2).

    Game script: each point of spread moves pass volume about 0.8% (underdogs throw more, favorites run more), capped at ±8%. The total sets overall play volume: (total ÷ league average)0.3.

    Player props: the consensus line for receiving yards, rushing yards, receptions, pass attempts, pass yards and anytime TD is treated as a market expectation:
    • Yardage medians get +5% for right skew, then shift by the no-vig over probability.
    • Anytime TD converts to expected TDs as −ln(1 − p).
    Each posted prop is blended 50/50 with the model's matching component. When market-implied points differ from the model by 15% or more (and at least 2 points), the player is flagged "Market higher" or "Market lower".

    2 · Opportunity

    Volume is a recency-weighted per-game average: each week back counts 0.7× as much, so the recent role drives the number. From play-by-play, the app computes:
    • Target share, air-yards share and WOPR (1.5 × target share + 0.7 × air-yards share)
    • RB opportunity share: carries + targets as a share of team carries + targets
    • Red-zone opportunity share inside the 20 and inside the 10
    • Designed QB runs and scrambles, tracked separately
    Snap share comes from snap counts. Not public: true route participation and RB red-zone snap share need player-tracking data that isn't released. TPRR and YPRR are estimated with routes ≈ team dropbacks × snap share, and are labeled "est."

    3 · Efficiency & over-expected

    The efficiency metrics come from four sources:
    • EPA per dropback, per carry and per target, plus CPOE, from play-by-play
    • Rush yards over expected per attempt, time to throw, separation and YAC over expected, from Next Gen Stats
    • Broken tackles per touch from Pro Football Reference, used as the missed-tackles-forced proxy
    • Expected fantasy points (xFP) and fantasy points over expected (FPOE) from ffopportunity
    TD rates are blended 50/50 toward expected TDs, so touchdown luck regresses. Players with at least 9 xFP per game who are running at least 2.5 points per game under expected are flagged as positive-regression candidates. Players at least 3 points per game (and 25%) over expected are flagged as fades.

    4 · Context & opponent

    Team context from play-by-play:
    • Neutral pass rate: win probability 20–80%, downs 1–3, outside the last two minutes of each half
    • Pass rate over expected
    • Neutral pace in seconds per play
    Pro Football Reference adds pressure rate allowed by each offense and generated by each defense, and Next Gen Stats adds QB time to throw. Opponent defenses still apply allowed-rate multipliers (yards per carry, yards per attempt, completion %, TD rates, attempts faced, fantasy points allowed to the position), regressed toward 1.0 for sample size.

    Distribution, boom & bust

    The weekly outcome is a normal distribution centered on the projection. Its spread starts from the player's week-to-week volatility blended with a position prior, then widens by 1.25×. The floor is a bad-week score the player finishes below about 10% of the time. The ceiling is a great-week score they top about 10% of the time, so roughly 80% of weeks land between them. Scores can be negative, which a gamma could not represent.

    Boom and bust thresholds in PPR:
    • QB: boom 25+, bust under 12
    • RB and WR: boom 20+, bust under 8
    • TE: boom 15+, bust under 5
    Non-QB thresholds drop 10% in Half-PPR and 20% in Standard.

    Weather

    For outdoor stadiums, the game-time forecast (kickoff through the next three hours) is pulled live from Open-Meteo once it's within about 15 days. Domes and retractable roofs get no adjustment. Wind is the biggest factor: passing and receiving production is trimmed slightly from 10 mph, more steeply from 15 mph, and heavily past 20 mph (about −11% at 20 mph, −17% at 25). Rain trims passing about 4% (9% if heavy), heavy snow about 15%, and temperatures at or below 20°F about 3%. Bad weather also adds a few percent to rushing attempts and raises fumble risk. The point change shows next to each forecast.

    Data

    Stats come from the open-source nflverse releases: weekly player and team stats, play-by-play, snap counts, Next Gen Stats, Pro Football Reference advanced stats, schedule lines and injury reports. Expected points come from ffopportunity. Spreads, totals and player-prop lines come from DraftKings through ESPN's public odds feed. A free GitHub Actions workflow rebuilds all data every 3 hours. ESPN's feed has no anytime-TD prices, so the anytime-TD market shows "No line" when that's the source. Any metric that isn't available shows as "Unavailable" or "No line". Generated . Projections cover regular-season games only; byes show as no game.

    My Team reads leagues read-only from Sleeper's public API and ESPN's public-league API, straight from your browser. Nothing is stored on a server; your league link lives in the page URL so you can bookmark it.
